Extractor rules demo 0.5.1: curated regex adaptations

Gitleaks
https://github.com/gitleaks/gitleaks/tree/b58d3f102cf3a2c84cb7f923d05c25c9b1aed84b
Source: config/gitleaks.toml. MIT license: Gitleaks-MIT.txt.
Adapted AWS, GitHub, Slack, Stripe and private-key patterns to Java regex.
Entropy checks, allowlists, keyword prefilters and upstream execution behavior are not imported.

Sensitive Discoverer
https://github.com/PortSwigger/sensitive-discoverer/tree/08fe769301487cfd9ac7ea44d72eff043271c8a5
Sources: src/main/resources/regexes/regex_general.jsonc and regex_token.jsonc.
Apache-2.0 license: SensitiveDiscoverer-Apache-2.0.txt.
Generic API-key/secret capture groups and private IPv4 ranges/octet boundaries were adapted.
Upstream refiner behavior is not imported. Modified patterns are opt-in templates, not equivalent detectors.

Presidio
https://github.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/tree/d4ccc6bcb6b6a7144336fe93b7839e61e251301b
Source: presidio-analyzer/presidio_analyzer/predefined_recognizers/generic/email_recognizer.py.
MIT license: Presidio-MIT.txt. Email pattern adapted with ASCII domain/boundary restrictions.
No NLP, confidence scoring, recognizer validation or TLD validation imported.

KR phone patterns and quoted-digit/adjacent-label/field-password examples are Burp Workbench adaptations or original examples.
libphonenumber research informs the KR pattern, but rules do not execute number validation.
These templates identify shapes/context chosen by the user; they do not guarantee personal-data or credential accuracy.
